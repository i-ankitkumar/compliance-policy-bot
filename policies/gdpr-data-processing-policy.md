# GDPR Data Processing Policy

## Purpose

Describes how the organization applies GDPR (and equivalent frameworks such
as the German BDSG / DSGVO) principles to the processing of personal data,
for both the organization's own employees and its customers' data subjects.

## Lawful Basis

Every processing activity involving personal data must have a documented
lawful basis under Article 6 (such as consent, contract necessity, legal
obligation, or legitimate interest). Processing special category data
(Article 9) additionally requires an explicit condition, such as explicit
consent or substantial public interest, and is logged in the Record of
Processing Activities (RoPA).

## Data Subject Rights

Data subjects may exercise the right of access, rectification, erasure,
restriction, portability, and objection. Requests are verified for identity,
then fulfilled within 30 calendar days, extendable by a further 60 days for
complex requests with notice to the data subject explaining the delay.
Erasure requests are honored unless a legal retention obligation under the
Data Retention Policy applies, in which case the data is restricted rather
than deleted and the requester is informed why.

## Data Protection by Design and Default

New systems or features that process personal data undergo a Data
Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) when the processing is likely to result
in high risk to data subjects, such as large-scale profiling or processing
of special category data. Data minimization is applied by default: only the
fields required for the stated purpose are collected.

## International Transfers

Transfers of personal data outside the EEA rely on an adequacy decision,
Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs), or another valid Article 46 transfer
mechanism, together with a documented transfer impact assessment where SCCs
are used. A current list of cross-border data flows is maintained alongside
the vendor inventory required by the Third-Party Risk Policy.

## Breach Notification

A personal data breach is handled under the Incident Response Policy, which
governs the 72-hour supervisory authority notification deadline and data
subject notification for high-risk breaches.

## Accountability

The Data Protection Officer maintains the RoPA, reviews DPIAs, and reports
data protection risk to leadership at least quarterly. Employees handling
personal data complete data protection training annually.
